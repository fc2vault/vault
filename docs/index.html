What it does

A wall, a player,
subtitles, a catalog.

Everything a tube site does for its own videos, done for the files on your disk. The filesystem is the source of truth; Vault never moves a file without a preview and never deletes one without your say‑so.

Browse

Poster wall with real filters

Grid, by‑actress and list views. Filter by watched state, favorites, wishlist, HD, uncensored, studio, resolution, duration, cup size and age. Full‑text search across code, title, tags and actress names, aliases and Japanese included.

Watch

Streams straight from the share

HTTP range requests and sendfile, so seeks over SMB are instant. Multi‑part movies auto‑advance. HEVC plays natively where the browser can, with a clear fallback where it can't. Keyboard shortcuts for everything.

Scenes

Markers on the seek bar

Scenes sit on the timeline as chips you can hover and click, tube‑site style. A fine‑tuned video model places them for you, offline, and every improved build is a free update.

Catalog

Actresses with faces and stats

Every performer gets an overview: portrait, Japanese name, aliases, body stats, cup size, and her filmography with the titles you don't own yet as a wishlist. Reassign a misgrouped movie with one click.

Organize

Tidy the disk, safely

Normalize folders to <Actress>/<CODE>/<CODE>.mp4, quarantine junk, flag duplicates. Always a preview first. Deleted movies go to a recoverable trash, never to rm.

Anywhere

One page, phone to desktop

The same page adapts to a phone over your LAN or VPN: two‑column wall, a filter drawer, a stacked player. No separate app and nothing to install on the phone.

Subtitles

English subtitles from the Japanese audio

Whisper writes down what is said, a small local model translates it with the conversation as context, and the lines land as .en.srt next to the movie. Runs offline on your machine; moans are left out, quiet words under them are not.

Import

From the download folder in one step

Recognises each release by its code (FC2 PPV and OMU, 10musume, Tokyo‑Hot, studio codes), renames it to <CODE>/<CODE>.mp4, drops spam clips and junk, moves it into the library and replaces an older copy with a bigger one — after a preview — then fetches the new movies' metadata by itself.

Metadata

Titles, casts and covers, filled in

fc2ppv‑db, fc2db.net, 123av and ffjav in priority order, each only filling what the ones before it left empty. Your own edits are never overwritten.

Nightly

Looks after the library while you sleep

In the hours you pick, Vault imports new downloads, fetches metadata for the new movies, places scene markers and writes subtitles. Whatever doesn't fit in one night is picked up the next.

Updates

New versions in one click

Vault tells you when there is a new version and installs it from Settings → Updates, checked against GitHub's checksum. Your library and settings stay; a newer catalog is merged into yours.

HTTPS

Encrypted on your network

One button makes Vault's own certificate and switches HTTPS on, no restart. Each phone trusts it once; the steps for iPhone, Android, Mac and Windows are in the app.

The player

Scene chips above the seek bar, English subtitles on the picture (CC, or v; Y / C jump between lines), tags and metadata beside it. Filmography of the performer on the left, so the next title is one click away.

The catalog

Portrait, Japanese name, measurements and aliases from the sources you enable, and everything you own by her in one place. Favorite, rename or merge from the same card.

On the phone

Same page, two columns.

Open Vault's address from any phone on your network and you get a two‑column wall, a filter drawer and a stacked player. Nothing to install on the phone, and nothing leaves your LAN.

Screenshots on this page come from a real library. Poster and video imagery is pixelated; the interface is as shipped.

The scene model

Trained on real reviews.
Updated every round.

Vault places scene markers itself, with a video classifier fine‑tuned on thousands of hand‑reviewed scene markers. It watches a movie in 6.4‑second windows and proposes where each act starts. Ten labels: blowjob, fingering, titjob, toy, creampie and the positions missionary, doggy, cowgirl, standing, scoop‑up. Toy, standing and creampie are new: the off‑the‑shelf model it started from could not see them at all. The markers land on the seek bar, one per change of action.

The model is actively maintained: each round is trained on a larger reviewed set and measured against the same held‑out performers, so every release is a verified step up. The newest build is a free download in Settings → Scene model, and the app tells you when there is a new one.

One Set up click installs what it needs to run, then Place markers works through the library — or a movie at a time, or each night for new arrivals. It runs offline on a Mac with Apple Silicon or any machine with an NVIDIA GPU. Nothing from your library leaves your network.

Privacy

Nothing leaves
your machine.

No cloud

A single Python process serves a single HTML page from your machine. Your library index, posters and catalog live in a folder next to it.

No account

Nothing to register. The only outbound traffic is the metadata scan you start yourself and the model downloads you ask for (the scene model, and the speech and translation models the first time you transcribe). Audio and subtitles never leave your machine.

No telemetry

No analytics, no crash reports. Vault asks GitHub once a day whether a newer version is out (one switch in Settings turns that off), and for a newer scene model when you open its tab — nothing about you or your library goes along. The source is open; read the whole server in one sitting.

LAN only

Vault has no login on purpose. Settings → HTTPS turns on encryption with one click, but that is not a login: use it on a trusted home network or over a VPN, and don't port‑forward it.

Install

One download,
nothing else.

The Mac and Windows downloads bring everything Vault needs, its own Python and ffmpeg included: no Terminal, no packages, no admin rights. All you need besides is a modern browser. On Linux Vault runs from source.

Python and ffmpeg

Built into the Mac and Windows downloads. On Linux, or to run from source anywhere: Python 3.8+ and ffmpeg from your package manager (apt install ffmpeg, brew install ffmpeg).

FlareSolverr

Optional. fc2ppv‑db sits behind a Cloudflare check; FlareSolverr clears it for you. A separate third‑party program, usually run in Docker. Without it you can paste a browser session instead.

Scene model

Optional. A ~450 MB download from Settings → Scene model, then Set up (~1 GB) to place markers. Placing them needs Apple Silicon or an NVIDIA GPU; viewing markers needs nothing special.

Subtitles

Optional. Settings → Subtitles → Set up installs Whisper and the translator into their own environment (~1 GB); the models (~4 GB) download on first use. Fastest on Apple Silicon or an NVIDIA GPU; on Windows / Linux the translator runs in Ollama or LM Studio.

Download

Vault for Apple Silicon (M1 and later, ~110 MB) or Vault for Intel (~120 MB). Not sure? Apple menu → About This Mac: "Chip: Apple M…" is Apple Silicon.

Drag it into Applications and open it

Open the downloaded file and drag Vault onto Applications. The first time you open it, macOS says it can't check the app for malware: Vault isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate. Click Done, then in System Settings → Privacy & Security scroll down and click Open Anyway. Only once.

Choose your movie folder

Vault asks for the folder that holds your movies, then opens in your browser. It runs while the app is open: click its Dock icon to bring the page back, quit it to stop. To have it start with your Mac, add Vault under System Settings → General → Login Items. From your phone: http://<this-mac>:8730/. New versions install from Settings → Updates.

Run the setup

Windows may say "Windows protected your PC": Vault isn't signed with a paid certificate. Click More info → Run anyway, only this once. Setup installs for your user only, no administrator rights; tick Start Vault when I log on to keep it running in the background.

Choose your movie folder

Vault asks for the folder that holds your movies, then opens in your browser. The Start menu has Vault (open it), Vault - Stop and Vault - Choose movie folder. Updating later: Settings → Updates installs a new version with one click, your settings stay.

Install ffmpeg

Python 3 is already there on every current distribution.

sudo apt install ffmpeg      # Debian / Ubuntu
sudo dnf install ffmpeg      # Fedora

Get Vault

Clone the repo and point it at your movies. Updating later is a git pull. (This also runs Vault from source on a Mac or a Windows PC.)

git clone https://github.com/fc2vault/vault.git
cd vault
python3 serve.py --library "/srv/movies"

Keep it running

Installs a systemd --user unit that starts at login and restarts on failure. On a headless server, also run loginctl enable-linger so it runs without a login session.

python3 serve.py --install-service
# then open http://<this-machine-ip>:8730/

Questions

Fair questions.

Is it really all free?

Yes. The app, every metadata source, the bundled catalog and the scene model, with no movie limit and no key. The terms are in LICENSE.md: use it, share it, don't sell it.

What do I get for supporting on Patreon?

The same Vault as everyone else, and my thanks. Contributions pay for the GPU time that trains each model round, the tooling and the hours behind every release. Nothing is held back from people who don't contribute.

How does the scene model get onto my machine?

Settings → Scene model shows the newest build and a Download button. Vault fetches it from the project's GitHub releases into a per‑user folder; Set up in the same tab installs what it needs to run (~1 GB), and Place markers does the rest. When a new round ships, the same tab offers the update.

Does the scene model need a GPU?

Tagging runs on Apple Silicon (MPS) at roughly 3× realtime, or much faster on any NVIDIA GPU. Viewing markers in the player needs nothing special. The model runs entirely on your machine.

Which sources does it use for metadata?

fc2db.net, 123av and ffjav work out of the box. fc2ppv‑db, which supplies the cast and faces, sits behind a Cloudflare check, so it needs a pasted browser session or a FlareSolverr instance on your LAN.

How good are the subtitles?

Good enough to follow the conversation. The Japanese transcript is usually accurate where people talk normally; quiet words under moaning are harder. In the English, expect roughly one line in five to be off, mostly where a word was misheard or who is talking to whom got mixed up. Each release improves the translator's glossary. Expect about 15–30 minutes per hour of video on an 8 GB M1 Mac, less on a faster machine. Everything runs locally: no audio and no text is sent anywhere.

Is this affiliated with FC2?

No. Vault is an independent, open‑source media manager for files you already own. It hosts nothing, links to no content, and includes no videos or images.
